A Boat Trip in Zadar With Kids: Which Tour, and What Actually Happens

Children are excellent on boats for about the first three hours and then they are finished, entirely and without warning. Everything below follows from that one observation. Pick the tour around it and a boat day with a family is the best day of the holiday; ignore it and you spend the last two hours negotiating.

Which tour suits which age

Under about six, take the half day. Four hours, two swim stops, back before the afternoon falls apart. It is the tour we recommend to families more than any other and it is not the expensive one.

From roughly seven upward the full day works, because by then a child can occupy themselves between stops and the eight hours stop feeling like a wait. The full day has lunch aboard, snorkelling gear and a paddleboard, and that last item does more for a nine year old than the entire rest of the itinerary.

Teenagers who want to actually sail rather than be transported are a different case. Put them on the winch and let them steer for a while, or look at the weekend course, where a maximum of six students share a boat with an instructor.

The sunset sail is three hours and looks family-friendly on paper. It is, for older children. For small ones it lands squarely on top of dinner and bedtime, and no view fixes that.

Morning, not afternoon

The half day runs morning or afternoon and with small children the morning is not a close call. It is cooler, the wind is lighter, the sea is flatter, and you are off the boat before the heat of the day rather than in the middle of it. The afternoon has livelier wind and warmer water, which is better for teenagers and worse for a four year old.

Where we actually stop

The half day makes two stops, chosen on the day from Galevac, Ošljak, Otrić and Kostanj bay depending on wind and sea. They are close in, sheltered, and none of them involves a long crossing to reach. Which two you get depends on the conditions rather than a brochure, which is exactly why they are all comfortable.

The full day goes further: the Tri Sestrice islets off Rivanj, where the water is clear enough that a snorkelling mask keeps children busy for an hour, then the old olive harbour at Muline on Ugljan, and a last swim in Frnaža bay.

The arithmetic that surprises families

Two adults and two children on the half day is four shared places at 75 EUR, so 300 EUR. The private half day starts at 400 EUR. With one more child or a grandparent you are at five people and 375 EUR, and at six you are past the private price and paying more for less privacy.

So: a family of four books shared places, and a family of six or more should ask for the private price first. On a private boat the route bends to you, which with children mainly means you can leave a bay the moment it stops being fun and stay in one that is working. The full price breakdown has the same arithmetic for every tour.

Life jackets, and what to tell us when you book

Every boat carries child size life jackets and the skippers are used to young crew. What we need from you is the ages, in the booking note. Not as a formality: it decides which jackets come aboard, and it changes how the skipper plans the stops.

What to bring, the short version

Sun cream applied before you arrive rather than on the pier, hats that will survive wind, a long sleeved shirt for after midday, and water shoes if your children like climbing on rocks, because sea urchins live there. Bring a change of clothes; children get wetter than they plan to.

Snacks are worth carrying even on the full day where lunch is included, because children do not eat on the schedule the boat does. The full packing list covers the rest, and the honest answer on safety covers what we cancel for and why.

The parts nobody mentions

There is one toilet aboard and it is small. Take everyone before you leave the marina.

Boats have no shade unless someone rigs it, so the cockpit is sunny for most of the day. That is manageable with a hat and a shirt and unmanageable without.

And the swim stops are the tour, as far as a child is concerned. The sailing between them is scenery. Once you accept that, you book the tour with the most swimming and the least transit, which is the morning half day, and everyone including you has a better day for it.

If you are choosing dates as well as tours, the month by month guide is worth a look: June and September give you the same sea as August with noticeably fewer people in the bays.
