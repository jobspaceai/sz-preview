Telašćica: Cliffs, a Salt Lake and the Best-Protected Bay in the Adriatic

Telašćica is a trick of geography: a bay ten kilometres deep cut into the southern end of Dugi Otok, so sheltered that charts mark it as a natural harbour for gales from any direction. Sailors have hidden here for two thousand years. Now it is a nature park, and hiding here is nicer than ever.

The cliffs

Walk twenty minutes up from the bay and the island simply ends: the Grpašćak cliffs fall 160 metres straight into open sea. Peregrine falcons nest in the face. Sunset from the top, with the Kornati scattered to the south, is the single best view in the Zadar archipelago, and most visitors on day boats never have time for it.

The salt lake

Behind the beach at Mir sits a lake the sea feeds through cracks in the limestone. It is warmer than the sea, saltier than the sea, and floating in it takes no effort at all. Try it in the early morning before the day boats arrive, when the only sound is donkeys arguing somewhere in the olives.

Anchoring and the night

The park has mooring buoys in several arms of the bay. Once the excursion traffic leaves, Telašćica gets so quiet you can hear fish break the surface. On our three-day route this is where we spend the second night, and it is the night guests talk about at breakfast.

Getting there

There is no good way to see Telašćica by car; the bay rewards boats. From Zadar it pairs naturally with Kornati, since the park border is a few hundred metres of water. Come for the day on a fast boat, or properly, overnight, under sail.
