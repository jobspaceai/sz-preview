The Sea Organ in Zadar: 35 Pipes, Seven Chords, and Hitchcock’s Favourite Sunset

In May 1964, Alfred Hitchcock checked into room 204 of the Hotel Zagreb on Zadar's waterfront. The master of suspense spent his evenings watching the sun go down over the channel, and left the city with a line the tourist board has been quoting ever since: "Zadar has the most beautiful sunset in the world, more beautiful than the one in Key West, Florida, applauded at every evening." He saw it from a hotel window. You can do better.

An instrument played by the sea

Forty-one years after Hitchcock's visit, in April 2005, the architect Nikola Bašić finished rebuilding a stretch of that same waterfront, and hid an instrument inside it. Under a run of wide white marble steps descending into the sea sit 35 organ pipes. As waves roll in, they push air through channels beneath the steps and out through whistle-holes in the promenade above. The pipes are tuned to seven chords of five tones, so whatever the sea plays is always, somehow, in harmony.

The result is the Morske orgulje, the Sea Organ: a musical instrument the size of a waterfront, playing continuously since the day it opened. A calm sea produces long, meditative chords. A ferry's wake sets off a sudden chorus. No two minutes are ever the same, which is why people who planned to stay five minutes are still sitting there an hour later. In 2006 the project won the European Prize for Urban Public Space, and it turned a plain concrete shoreline into the most loved few metres of the city.

When to go, and the problem with going

Sunset, obviously. The afternoon breeze usually gives the organ its liveliest voice just as the light turns gold, and a few steps away Bašić's other installation, the Greeting to the Sun, starts glowing underfoot. That is also the problem: in July and August the steps are packed shoulder to shoulder, and the world's most beautiful sunset comes with an audience of hundreds. Come at six in the morning and the instrument is yours alone. Or do what locals with boats do.

The version almost nobody hears

From the water, thirty metres off the steps, the Sea Organ is a different instrument. The sound reaches you rounder and quieter, mixed with the small noises of the city behind it, and the crowd on the steps becomes part of the view instead of your seat neighbours. Our evening sail idles past the organ exactly at golden hour, then turns west into the channel. The sun drops behind Ugljan, there is local wine in your glass, and somewhere behind you the sea keeps playing to an audience that never claps, because the organ never stops for applause.

Hitchcock had room 204. You will have the whole channel.
