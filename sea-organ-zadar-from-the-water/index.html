The Sea Organ in Zadar: Why You Should Hear It from a Boat

At the tip of Zadar’s old town, a set of white marble steps runs into the sea. Under them, thirty-five organ pipes turn every wave into sound. This is the Morske orgulje, the Sea Organ, and it has been playing without pause since 2005. Alfred Hitchcock already knew the spot: he called Zadar’s sunset the most beautiful in the world half a century before the organ was built under it.

How it works

Waves push air through channels under the steps; the pipes do the rest. Calm sea, long meditative chords. Ferry wake, a sudden chorus. No two minutes repeat, which is why people who planned to stay five minutes are still sitting there an hour later.

When to go

Sunset, obviously, and that is also the problem: in July and August the steps are shoulder to shoulder. Come at 06:00 and you will have the instrument to yourself. Or do what locals with boats do.

The version almost nobody hears

From the water, thirty metres off the steps, the organ sounds different: rounder, quieter, mixed with the city noise fading behind it. Our sunset sail idles past the organ exactly at golden hour, then turns west into the channel so you watch Hitchcock’s sunset drop behind Ugljan with a glass of local wine, and the crowd on the steps becomes part of the view instead of your seat neighbours.
