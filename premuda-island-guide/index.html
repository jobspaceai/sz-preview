Premuda: the Cathedral Under the Sea and the Titanic of the Adriatic

Premuda is the outermost inhabited island of the Zadar archipelago, a nine-kilometre sliver of stone facing the open Adriatic with almost nobody on it. It is also, by some distance, the most famous piece of seabed in Croatia.

The Cathedral

Off the island's coast lies a system of underwater caves and passages that divers have named the Cathedral. The name is descriptive rather than romantic: sunlight enters through openings in the rock above and falls in columns through the blue, and the chambers are high enough that divers speak in the same slightly hushed way people do in churches. It is one of the best-known dive sites on this coast, and it requires a guide and proper equipment.

Szent István

Four kilometres off Premuda, at 66 metres, lies the wreck that gives the island its second reputation. The Austro-Hungarian battleship Szent István was torpedoed here in June 1918 by an Italian motor torpedo boat and capsized within hours. She lies upside down on the seabed, was rediscovered in the mid-1970s, and is now a protected site under the Croatian Ministry of Culture. Divers call her the Titanic of the Adriatic; the comparison is about scale and about the fact that the sinking was filmed, one of the few battleship losses of the First World War captured on camera.

Above the water

The island itself has one village, a small harbour, no traffic and a permanent population in the dozens. Its beaches are white pebble, its water is famously clear because there is nothing on the island to make it otherwise, and in September it is close to empty.

Getting there

Premuda is a long way out: this is a speedboat destination or a leg of a multi-day charter, not a casual afternoon. Diving the wreck requires a licensed operator and deep-diving certification; the Cathedral needs a guide. But you can anchor above both, swim in water that has nothing upstream of it, and know exactly what is down there.
