Why the sailing season here starts when the tourists leave

July is a thermal month. The maestral fills in around lunchtime, blows force 3 to 5 for a few hours and dies at sunset. Pleasant, predictable, and not much of a race. From September onward that pure thermal pattern gives way to weather systems with more in them, and the wind holds for longer parts of the day.

The sea does not cool the way people assume. It is at its warmest of the entire year in early September, after three months of absorbing heat, and it stays above 20 degrees through much of October. Going over the side in October here is unpleasant rather than dangerous.

The bays are empty and so are the berths. In August you plan a route around where you can tie up. In late September you tie up where you like. None of this is a discount argument. It is simply better sailing, in the months when the archipelago is quiet.

Level 1

Regatta programme: the whole boat

For a club, a company or a group of friends arriving at a regatta without a boat. You get the yacht, the sails and the equipment, and a local skipper if you want one. Everything unnecessary comes off the boat before the start, which on a cruising yacht is a great deal more than people expect.

The local skipper is the part that is hard to buy elsewhere. Someone who has raced this water knows where the wind bends around Ugljan, which side of the channel pays on a northwesterly, and how the current behaves in the Ždrelac passage. Over a weekend that is worth more than any sail wardrobe.

We can also equip a whole club or company arriving with several crews. Three of the boats are ours; further yachts come from partner fleets on request. The exact number and type are agreed for each regatta date, because it depends on availability, so we do not promise a fleet size in advance.

What this covers

  • Yacht prepared for racing, with the cruising inventory left ashore
  • Local skipper who knows the course area, optional
  • A group of boats for a club championship, a corporate regatta or a friendly race between two teams
  • Entry, crew list and paperwork handled with the organiser
  • Berth in Marina Zadar before and after the event

Level 2

Guest on a regatta, no experience needed

German speaking sailors call this Mitsegeln, and the phrase most of them type into a search box is Hand gegen Koje. Be careful with that term: in its original meaning it usually describes a free berth, or one shared against running costs, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. Here it is a place in a racing crew, and what it costs is agreed on request. Everything else about it is exactly what the term promises.

You do not need a licence and you do not need experience. You get a job on the boat on the first morning, someone shows you how to do it, and by the second start you are doing it without being told. Most guests end up on the rail, on a winch or on the bow, depending on how much they mind being wet.

What is expected of you, honestly: early starts, often before seven. Wet, because a racing boat has no bimini and spray goes where it likes. Physical work in short bursts, separated by long flat periods of sitting on the high side doing nothing at all. And doing what the helm says immediately, without discussion, because on a start line there is no time for a conversation.

What you get: a real race instead of a tour of the bays. A briefing beforehand where the plan is explained in language you understand, and a debrief afterwards where the mistakes are gone through, the skipper’s included. A berth aboard on multi day events.

This suits you if

  • You want to sail properly rather than be driven around
  • You are fit enough for a long day and do not mind being cold and wet for part of it
  • You would rather learn by doing a job badly at first than by watching someone else do it well
  • It does not suit you if you want a quiet swimming holiday. That is what the day trips are for.

Level 3

Experienced sailor

For someone who already sails and wants competitive sailing rather than a delivery. It can be a single training day, a run of days before an event, or a full regatta with a crew we build around you.

The work is the boring, decisive part: getting the main to the right shape for the wind you actually have, not the wind you had ten minutes ago. Headsail trim through a shift. The five minutes before a start, which decide more races here than the beat that follows them.

The course area rewards local knowledge. The islands throw wind shadows longer than they look, the channels accelerate a northwesterly, and taking the wrong side of Ugljan on the wrong day costs you the leg. Helm time is part of it. You steer, and somebody experienced sits next to you and tells you what you are doing wrong.

Usually covered

  • Sail trim on a performance cruiser rather than a charter boat with an in mast furler
  • Start procedure, timing and line bias
  • Tactics for the Zadar channel and the island wind shadows
  • Crew work: manoeuvres repeated until they stop being an event
  • Helm time under pressure

Boat rental for the regatta

Three yachts are ours and we know exactly what they have and what they do not. Beyond those three, further boats come from partner fleets in the Zadar area. Their type and size are agreed with your enquiry rather than listed here, because we do not have the same first hand knowledge of them.

The Salona 44 is the one to look at first. Salona yachts are built in Solin, on this coast, for this sea, and Croatian crews know the boat before they step aboard. She is a performance cruiser rather than a charter hull with an apologetic keel, and she comes alive as soon as there is some weight in the wind.

Boats go out with a skipper or without one. Bare charter needs a valid licence and a VHF certificate, and the paperwork is checked before the boat leaves rather than after.

Questions people actually ask

Do I need a licence or experience to join a regatta as crew?

No. On the guest level you arrive with no licence and no sailing hours, you get a job on the boat, and you learn it during the event. What you do need is a reasonable level of fitness, a willingness to be wet and cold for parts of the day, and the ability to do what the helm says without arguing about it on a start line.

What does Hand gegen Koje mean on this page?

It is the German term people search for, and we use it because it is what our guests call this. In its original meaning it often describes a free berth, or one shared against running costs. That is not what is being promised here. This is a place in a racing crew, and the terms are agreed on request, in writing, before you book anything.

Are your boats ORC measured?

We do not claim a valid ORC certificate for any boat on this page. Certificates carry dates and they expire. Tell us the boat and the event and we will check the current status and tell you honestly what is valid and what would still need doing.

How many boats can you provide for one regatta?

Three of the yachts are ours. Additional boats come from partner fleets in the Zadar area. The number and the types depend on the date, so we confirm a fleet against a specific regatta rather than promising one in advance. Clubs arriving with several crews are exactly the case this is built for.

When is the best time for racing here?

September and October. The wind is steadier than in midsummer, because the afternoon thermal gives way to real weather systems, the sea is still above 20 degrees, and the anchorages and marinas are empty. July is warmer and considerably less interesting to sail in.
