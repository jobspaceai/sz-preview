Kornati Islands from Zadar: What a Sailing Day Really Looks Like

George Bernard Shaw supposedly said God made the Kornati out of tears, stars and breath. Skippers say something less poetic: there is no water, no shade and no shop, and that is exactly the point. The Kornati are what the Adriatic looked like before anyone built anything on it.

What the park actually is

Kornati National Park covers 89 islands, islets and reefs southwest of Zadar. Most are bare limestone, streaked silver and gold, dropping into some of the clearest water in Croatia. A handful of stone cottages and konobas sit in the bays, run by families from Murter who have grazed sheep and fished here for generations.

The sailing

From Zadar you enter the park after two to three hours of sailing, most of it in the lee of Dugi Otok, so the sea stays comfortable even when the maestral is working. Inside the park the channels are narrow and the wind bends around the islands. It is concentration sailing, the kind our guests end up loving most, and the skipper will hand you the wheel in the wider passages if you want it.

Where you stop

Anchorages we use depend on the wind: Levrnaka for the sand-bottomed bay under the tallest cliffs, Ravni Žakan when we want the konoba, Lavsa for the quietest night. Dinner is grilled fish and lamb at a family konoba, reachable only by boat, paid in cash and remembered for years.

Day trip or overnight?

You can touch the edge of the park on a long day sail. To actually feel it, sleep inside it. The moment the excursion boats leave around 17:00, the park empties, the light goes gold and you share ninety islands with a dozen sailboats. That evening is the reason our three-day trip exists.
