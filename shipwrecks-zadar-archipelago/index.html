The Shipwrecks of the Zadar Archipelago: Four Ships, Four Stories

Every stretch of busy sea keeps a record of its accidents, and the Zadar archipelago is no different. What makes this particular seabed unusual is the range: within a couple of hours' sailing you can pass over a First World War battleship lying at 66 metres, a cargo ship that sank in living memory, a freighter that ran aground in the 1980s, and a bay where two wrecks sit in water shallow enough to stand in.

Szent István, 1918: the deep one

The Austro-Hungarian battleship Szent István was torpedoed off Premuda in June 1918 and capsized within hours. She lies upside down at 66 metres, was rediscovered in the mid-1970s, and is a protected site: technical divers only, with permits. For everyone else, it is a place to anchor above and think about.

Ledenik, 2008: the one you can snorkel

A refrigerated cargo ship built in 1962 and registered in Zadar, the Ledenik sank on 23 October 2008 about two hundred metres off Sestrunj. She rests at around twenty metres with her shallowest structure at ten, and on a calm day her outline is visible from the surface. This is the archipelago's most accessible real wreck: no decompression, no certification, just a mask and reasonable fitness.

Michele, 1983: the freighter on the sand

On 11 June 1983 the Italian commercial ship Michele, 76 metres long, ran aground on a sandy bottom off the northern cape of Dugi Otok. She has been a snorkelling and shallow-dive site ever since, broken up by four decades of weather but still unmistakably a ship.

Lučica: two wrecks, four metres of water

And then there is Lučica bay near Soline on Dugi Otok, where a Second World War cargo ship hit by an Italian bomber lies in two to four metres of water, with a Croatian passenger vessel in the shallows on the other side of the bay. Two wrecks, one bay, no diving gear required.

What you can actually see, and how

Snorkelling depth: Lučica and Michele. Snorkelling with confidence: Ledenik. Certified diving: Ledenik properly, and the Cathedral caves at Premuda. Technical diving with permits: Szent István only.

Most of these sites are reachable on a speedboat day or a multi-day sail. None of them are on a bus route, which is exactly why they are still worth seeing.
