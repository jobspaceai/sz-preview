Fort St Michael: the 13th-Century Watchtower With a View of 200 Islands

There is a hill above the village of Preko on Ugljan with a ruined fortress on top, and from up there you can see more than two hundred islands. On a clear day the view runs from the mainland mountains to the outer edge of the Kornati. It is the single best vantage point in the Zadar archipelago, and people have understood that for about fifteen hundred years.

Why anyone built here

Position. From this hill you control the sea approaches to Zadar: nothing crosses the channel without being seen. The first known references tie the site to a Benedictine monastery of St Michael from the late 11th or first half of the 12th century, and the medieval castle went up in the 13th century, probably on the footprint of a much older fortification from the 6th.

It earned its keep. During the Venetian sieges of Zadar in 1345 and 1346, this fortress mattered: whoever held the hill could watch, and interfere with, everything moving in the city's waters.

What is up there now

Ruins, a chapel, and the view. The walls are partly standing, the site is open, and the walk up from Preko takes around an hour on a marked path through macchia and olive terraces, or you can drive most of the way. Go early or late; there is no shade at the top and the stone holds the afternoon heat.

How it fits a day on the water

From a boat, Fort St Michael is the landmark you steer by: the notch on Ugljan's ridge that tells you where Preko is. Our day sailing route passes below it on the way out, and the half-day trip swims at Galevac and Ošljak, the islets directly beneath the hill.

If you have a spare morning on land before or after your sail, take the ferry to Preko and climb it. Then go sailing, and spend the day recognising, from sea level, every island you looked down on that morning.
