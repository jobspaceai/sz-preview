Iž: the Only Adriatic Island Famous for Pottery

Every island in this archipelago lived off the same three things: olives, sheep and fish. Iž added a fourth, and it made the island's name. Here they made pots.

A clay economy

The first written record of Iž pottery dates to 1530, and for centuries afterwards the island's wood-fired pots travelled to markets across the Adriatic, along the coast, out to other islands and deep into the mainland interior. It was not decorative work: these were everyday objects, water jars, cooking pots, bowls, and they were good enough that people who could have bought local instead bought from Iž.

The craft nearly died in the twentieth century, as crafts do, and a handful of potters keep it alive today. In Veli Iž the ethnographic collection holds local and imported ceramics alongside costumes, weaving tools, wool, linen and cotton handicrafts and shoemaking gear: an honest picture of what an island economy looked like before tourism.

Sailing to Iž

Iž lies between Ugljan and Dugi Otok, in the middle of the archipelago, which makes it a natural stop on a longer day or a private charter. The coast is indented and gentle, the anchorages are shallow and sheltered, and Veli Iž has a small marina if you want to walk ashore for an hour. There are no crowds here in any month, which is the point.

Bring back a pot if you can find one. It will be the only souvenir on your shelf with a five-hundred-year supply chain behind it.
