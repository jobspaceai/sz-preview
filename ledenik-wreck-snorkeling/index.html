The Ledenik: a Whole Ship You Can See Without Diving Gear

Most shipwreck stories require a diving licence to finish. This one does not.

23 Oct 2008date of sinking
~20 mdepth of the wreck
10 mshallowest point

What happened

The Ledenik was a refrigerated cargo ship, a working boat rather than anything glamorous: built in 1962, registered in Zadar, used for carrying catch. On 23 October 2008 she was returning from fishing grounds when she went down roughly two hundred metres from the shore of Sestrunj, near Hrvatin cove. Nobody sings songs about her. She simply sank, close to shore, in shallow water, and stayed there.

In the weeks that followed, islanders reported an oil sheen near the wreck, and the question of salvage went back and forth. She was never raised.

Why she is worth a stop

Because of the depth. The wreck sits at about twenty metres, with her shallowest structure roughly ten metres below the surface, in water clear enough that the hull's outline shows through from above on a calm day. For a certified diver that makes her a straightforward, sunlit dive with no decompression obligation. For a snorkeller with reasonable breath-holding it makes her something rarer: a real, whole shipwreck you can look down on, and swim over, without a tank.

Marine life has done what marine life does. Fifteen-plus years underwater is long enough for the structure to have become a reef, and the fish know it.

Practical notes

The site is reachable only by boat, roughly half an hour out of Zadar at speedboat pace. Conditions matter more here than at most stops: you want a calm day, good light, and ideally midday sun. Currents around Sestrunj can be lively, so this is a stop to take with a skipper who knows the ground rather than a swim to improvise.

Ask us about adding it to a speedboat day or a private charter. It pairs naturally with the Tri Sestrice islets and the rest of our usual swim stops, and it is the only one where the thing you are swimming over used to have a crew.
