When to Sail in Zadar: a Month-by-Month Guide

The Zadar sailing season runs from May to October, and the months are not interchangeable. People book July because that is when they have holiday, which is fair, but if your dates are actually flexible the calendar has a clear answer and it is not July. Here is what each month gives you, in sea temperature, wind, crowds and money.

May: cool sea, empty bays

The land is warm and the sea is not quite there yet, usually still in the high teens, which is swimmable if you are the sort of person who swims in the high teens and a genuine shock if you are not. Everything else is at its best. The islands are still green rather than burnt, the bays are empty, the light is clear rather than hazy, restaurants have time for you, and prices are lower across the board.

May is the month for sailing as an activity rather than as transport between swims. If the point of the day is hours in the water, wait. If the point is being on a boat in an archipelago with nobody else in it, this is arguably the best month of the year.

June: the sweet spot

Sea temperature catches up and crosses 20 degrees, the days are at their longest of the year, and the crowds have not arrived yet. The maestral, the afternoon thermal wind that makes sailing here reliable, settles into its proper summer pattern by mid-month.

Long daylight matters more than people expect. In late June you can leave at nine, spend eight hours on the water, and still have three hours of light left when you tie up, which turns a day trip into a whole day rather than the middle of one. If you want the postcard version of the archipelago without sharing it, June is the month.

July and August: hot, busy, alive

The warmest sea of the summer at around 25 degrees, the strongest sun, the fullest anchorages and the busiest town. Everything is open, every boat is out, and the archipelago is at maximum volume in both senses.

It is also when the islands have their own life rather than a version staged for visitors. Saljske užance in Sali, with its donkey races and donkey orchestra, runs on the weekend before 15 August. Village feast days fill the calendar. Konobas that shut in October are open every night.

The trade-offs are real and worth planning around. Book earlier than you think you need to, particularly for the sunset sail, which sells out first. Start earlier in the day than feels reasonable. Treat the middle of the day as swimming time rather than sightseeing time, because walking around a limestone island at two in the afternoon in August is a decision you make exactly once. And at Sakarun, go before eleven or after four, never in between.

September: the local favourite

The sea is at its warmest of the entire year in early September, having spent three months absorbing heat, while the crowds thin out from the first week onward as families go back to school. Air temperatures drop to the point where walking around an island is pleasant again. The wind often becomes more interesting for actual sailing, since the pure thermal pattern of midsummer starts giving way to systems with a bit more in them.

Almost everyone in this business, asked privately when they would take their own holiday here, says September. That is not a coincidence and it is not a secret, it is just that most people cannot move their holiday.

October: the long goodbye

Warm sea, soft light, quiet bays, and a rising chance that the weather has opinions. The sea holds above 20 degrees through much of the month, which surprises people who assume October means a cold Adriatic. What changes is reliability: the bura season begins, systems move through faster, and the forecast starts steering the schedule rather than decorating it.

Trips still run. Restaurants and shops on the smaller islands start closing from mid-month, so a route that assumes lunch ashore needs checking first. If your dates are flexible and you do not mind a change of plan, October hands you the archipelago almost to yourself.

What about the rest of the year

Between November and April the sea drops toward 12 degrees, the bura is at its most serious, and the tourist infrastructure on the islands is shut. Zadar itself is excellent in winter, and cheap, and empty in the good way. But this is not a sailing season, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling something.

A word about wind, in every month

Whether we sail or motor depends on the wind, always. The maestral fills in on summer afternoons and is a gift. The bura comes down cold and hard off the mainland mountains and is a reason to change plans. The jugo brings cloud and swell and takes a day to arrive, so you can see it coming. Our full guide to the three winds explains what the skipper is actually looking at on the morning of your trip.

The skipper decides on the day, always in favour of safety. If the weather makes a trip unsafe, we reschedule free of charge or refund in full. That policy exists in every month, and it gets used most often in the two at either end of the season.

Picking a month, briefly

For the warmest water and the most going on: late July through early September. For sailing and space: May, June or October. For the best compromise, and the one we would pick ourselves: the first three weeks of September, with June a close second.

Then look at what you want to do with the day. All of it works in all six months: the full day out to the islands, the half day, the evening sail past the old town, or the three-day route down to Telašćica and the Kornati. Pack according to our list, pick your month, and then let the sea have the last word. It will anyway.
