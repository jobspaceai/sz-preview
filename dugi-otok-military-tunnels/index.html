The Tunnels of Dugi Otok: Where a Navy Hid Its Ships Inside a Mountain

From the water, most of the coast of Dugi Otok reads as ordinary: rock, pine, the occasional cove. Then the cliff opens, and there is a rectangular hole in it, sized for something considerably bigger than a fishing boat. You can steer straight in.

Built to disappear

Most of the submarine tunnels along this coast were begun during the German occupation in the Second World War, when a sheltered Adriatic base that could not be photographed from the air was worth the enormous effort of cutting one out of solid stone. After the war the Yugoslav navy inherited them, and for close to fifty years these tunnels did exactly what they were designed for: a warship or submarine went in, and from above there was nothing to see.

They are not the only ones. Similar installations exist on Vis and in the Bay of Kotor, part of a Cold War coastline built to survive being looked at from space. The ones here are simply the ones you can reach on a day out from Zadar.

What it is like inside

Cold, first of all: the temperature drops the moment you pass the entrance. Then the acoustics, an engine at idle sounds like a cathedral organ. The walls carry tool marks and, in places, rails and fittings. Light comes in behind you and dies quickly, so the far end is a wall of dark that your eyes take a while to accept. It is one of the few genuinely strange experiences on this coast, and it is entirely free of interpretation panels, gift shops or queues.

How to see them

By boat, and preferably one with a skipper who knows which entrances are safe. Depths and debris vary, some tunnels are silted, and the entrances face the weather in ways that matter. Our speedboat trips can include a tunnel on a Dugi Otok route; on a multi-day sail it makes a memorable detour between Telašćica and the northern beaches.

Bring a light. Bring a jacket, seriously: the temperature difference surprises people every time. And when the engine goes quiet inside, listen, because the silence in there is about eighty years old.
