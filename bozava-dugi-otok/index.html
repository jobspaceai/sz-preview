Božava: a Pine Wood, a Harbour, and a Century of Quiet Tourism

On the northeastern coast of Dugi Otok, sheltered from the open sea by the whole width of the island, Božava is a small place with a long pine wood, a harbour, and a quiet claim: it was doing tourism on this island before almost anyone else.

Early adopters

Visitors began coming to Božava in the early twentieth century, when the notion of travelling to a small Dalmatian island for the air and the swimming was still novel. The pine wood behind the village, planted and tended, was part of the appeal, and it still is: it comes down to the water and throws shade over a coastal path that is one of the pleasantest walks on the island.

What Božava did not do is grow. It has a hotel or two, a harbour, a few restaurants and the same handful of streets, and the surrounding coast is still coves and rock rather than development.

What is around it

Božava sits within easy reach of the island's northern highlights: Sakarun beach is a short run west, the Veli Rat lighthouse marks the tip beyond it, and the big inlet of Luka Solišćica with the village of Soline cuts into the island just north. It also makes a sensible base for the western coast's caves when the weather allows.

On the water

The harbour is small but useful, and the bays either side are good anchorages in settled weather. On a speedboat day Božava is a natural lunch stop between Sakarun and the lighthouse; on a longer cruise it is a calm overnight with a pine wood to walk off dinner in.

There is no attraction here in the ticketed sense. That is precisely the attraction.
