Rava and Rivanj: Seven Square Kilometres, Fifteen Bays, Thirty-One Residents

Between the big names of the archipelago lie two islands most visitors never learn to pronounce, let alone visit. They are the reason the phrase "off the beaten track" still means something here.

Rava: an island that is mostly coastline

Rava lies in the Iž channel, between Iž and Dugi Otok, and covers around seven square kilometres. Into that it fits fifteen bays. The ratio tells you what the island is: a low, indented ridge of stone and pine where the sea reaches into the land from both sides and nothing is ever more than a few minutes' walk from water. There are no hotels, one small settlement split between two harbours, and a population you could seat in a village bar.

For a boat, that is close to ideal. Fifteen bays means that whatever the wind is doing, one of them is calm.

Rivanj: thirty-one people and an aquarium offshore

Rivanj is smaller still, 4.4 square kilometres, with 31 permanent residents at the last count. It sits between Sestrunj and Ugljan, and it matters to us for what lies just off it: the Tri Sestrice islets, the shallow, protected channels that make the first swim stop of our full-day route feel like snorkelling in an aquarium.

The island itself has no shops, no roads worth the name and no reason to hurry. Sheep still graze the terraces. In winter the population is a handful of families; in August it is a handful of families and their visiting grandchildren.

Why bother?

Because this is where the archipelago stops performing. On Dugi Otok you share a beach; here you share nothing. If you have a boat for the day and want to spend it somewhere that will not appear on anyone's feed, point at Rava and see what happens.
