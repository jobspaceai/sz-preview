Ugljan: the Island That Sold Olive Oil to the Roman Empire

Ugljan is the wall of green you see from Zadar's waterfront, close enough that the ferry takes twenty minutes and the lights of its villages are part of the city's night view. It is also the island our day sailing trip knows best, and the one with the oldest business record in the archipelago.

Muline: a Roman factory in a quiet bay

On the island's northern tip lies Muline, today a hamlet with a few houses and a jetty. In the first century AD it was one of the Mediterranean's serious olive oil producers. Archaeologists have uncovered a Roman villa rustica with five presses and estimated seasonal output at around 85,000 litres, sold across the empire as Liburnian oil and shipped in roughly 3,240 amphorae of 26 litres each. Around the mill lie a late antique mausoleum and the stone outlines of a three-nave early Christian basilica from the 5th century, still visible among the olives and cypresses. A faithful replica of the ancient mill was built beside the site in 2007.

We anchor off Muline for lunch on the full-day route. It is a good place to think about how little has changed: the same slopes still grow olives, and the oil still tastes like the reason someone built a factory here.

The bays

Ugljan's coast is folded into dozens of coves, and the good ones have no road. Frnaža, on the northeastern shore, is where we take the last long swim of the day: pebbles, pines and water clear to the anchor chain. Ćinta has a shallow sandy bottom that goes bath-warm by afternoon. Off Preko, the islets of Galevac and Ošljak are the usual stops on the half-day trip.

Why it works as a first sailing day

Because it is close. The channel between Zadar and Ugljan is sheltered, the crossing takes under an hour under sail, and if the wind dies you have lost nothing. Families with small children, guests who are unsure about seasickness, and anyone with an afternoon flight all end up here, and none of them feel short-changed: this is still the Adriatic that put the archipelago on postcards, only twenty minutes from a parking space in town.
