The Bays Near Zadar You Can Only Reach by Boat (One Comes With a Roman Factory)

Ask about beaches near Zadar and you will be sent to the city ones: fine for an afternoon, full by ten. The better answer lies across the channel. The islands facing the city, Ugljan, Rivanj and Sestrunj, are folded into dozens of coves that no bus can reach, and most of them have no name on the map apps. These are the stops our skippers actually steer for on the day sailing route, and each has its own story.

Tri Sestrice: the aquarium

The Three Sisters are a scatter of islets off Rivanj and the first stop of our full-day route. The channels between them are the closest thing this part of the Adriatic has to an aquarium: shallow, protected, and so clear you stop trusting your depth perception. We anchor in the lee, the SUP goes in the water, and this is reliably the stop where the schedule begins to slip. It never survives Tri Sestrice, and it is not supposed to.

Muline: lunch beside a Roman factory

Our lunch anchorage sits off Muline, a quiet hamlet on the northern tip of Ugljan, and here the story goes deep. In the first century AD this little bay was one of the Mediterranean's serious olive oil producers. Archaeologists have uncovered a Roman villa rustica with five oil presses that turned out an estimated 85,000 litres a season, oil famous in its day as Liburnian oil, shipped across the empire in some 3,240 amphorae of 26 litres each. Around the mill lie the remains of a late antique mausoleum and a three-nave early Christian basilica from the 5th century, its stone outlines still standing among the olives and cypresses. A faithful replica of the ancient mill was built beside the site in 2007. Two thousand years later the same slopes still grow olives, and you eat your lunch at anchor a stone's throw from where Rome bottled its finest.

Frnaža: the last long swim

On the northeast coast of Ugljan, Frnaža is the cove we save for the end of the day: pebbles, pines, and water clear to the anchor chain. On a weekday morning there is a fair chance you will share it with nobody at all. It is twenty minutes from the city at sailing speed, and it feels like another decade.

Ćinta and the surprise stop

Ćinta, with its shallow sandy bottom that goes bath-warm by afternoon, is often our surprise stop, the one the skipper adds between Tri Sestrice and Muline when wind and sea allow. Every crew gets a different one, and none of them are printed in any brochure. That is rather the point of having a boat.

How to reach them

Tri Sestrice, Muline and Frnaža are fixed points of our day sailing trip from Zadar; the surprise stop belongs to the wind. On a private charter, the whole route bends to your wishes. A boat does not follow a road, and in these islands, that is the entire idea.
