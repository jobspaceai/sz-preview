Pašman: Benedictines, and the Channel That Cleans Itself Twice a Day

South of Ugljan, joined to it by a short bridge, Pašman is the island people sail past on their way somewhere else. That is their loss, and quietly our gain: it is emptier, greener and, by a measurable margin, cleaner.

The channel that resets

The Pašman channel, the strip of sea between the island and the mainland, has an unusual habit: its current reverses roughly every six hours. Locals will tell you, with the confidence of people who swim in it daily, that this is why the water here is among the clearest on the Croatian coast; the channel simply flushes itself twice a day. Whether or not the hydrology is that tidy, the swimming is excellent, and divers rate the visibility.

Ćokovac: nine centuries of the same routine

On a hill above Tkon stands the Benedictine monastery of Ss Cosmas and Damian, on a site the order has held since the 12th century. It has been burnt, rebuilt, emptied and repopulated, and monks are there today keeping the same offices at the same hours their predecessors kept when the Kornati were still owned by Zadar nobles. Whatever you believe, an institution that has run continuously for nine hundred years on a small island is worth thirty minutes of your day.

How Pašman fits a sailing day

The island sits at the southern end of our usual cruising ground and works best on a private charter, where the route bends to your wishes and the day can run long. Its bays are shallow, sandy and well protected, and the villages, Tkon, Kraj, Pašman, Ždrelac, are working places rather than resorts: a bakery, a church, a few konobas, boats pulled up on the hard.

If you want the archipelago without a single souvenir shop, ask the skipper for Pašman.
