The Caves of Dugi Otok: an Emperor’s Excursion and a Hole in the Ceiling

The western coast of Dugi Otok faces the open Adriatic and takes the full weight of every storm that crosses it. Several thousand years of that produces cliffs, and cliffs produce caves. Two of them are worth planning a day around.

Strašna peć: the cave with imperial credentials

Near the village of Savar, on the island's southern half, is a cave called Strašna peć, roughly seven metres high and ten wide, hung with stalactites. Its claim to fame is not geological but social: it was the first organised excursion destination in this part of the Adriatic, and in 1904 the Austro-Hungarian emperor Franz Joseph came to see it.

Think about what that means. Before Sakarun, before the national parks, before any of the things visitors now come for, the thing worth showing an emperor on this island was a hole in the rock. Tourism on Dugi Otok started here.

Golubinka: the light through the roof

Golubinka is a sea cave you enter by swimming or by kayak, and its trick is overhead: an opening in the ceiling lets a shaft of daylight down into the chamber, which turns the water below an improbable colour and makes everyone reach for a phone they should have left on the boat. It is small, it is theatrical, and it is best in the middle of the day when the sun is high enough to drop straight through.

Brbišćica: the bay under the cliffs

On the same western shore lies Brbišćica, a bay ringed by high cliffs with more caves scattered along them. It is exposed, so it needs a settled day, but on that day it is one of the most dramatic anchorages in the archipelago: sheer rock on three sides, deep blue water, and nothing else at all.

Planning it

All three are boat-only, all three need calm weather, and the western coast is unforgiving when the wind turns. That makes them a speedboat or private charter proposition with a skipper watching the forecast. Combine the caves with Sakarun and the Veli Rat lighthouse and you have the northern half of the island in a single, very full day.
