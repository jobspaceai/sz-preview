Sakarun Beach: Croatia’s White-Sand Bay, and the Lighthouse Built on Egg Yolks

Every summer the photos of Sakarun do the rounds: a crescent of white sand on Dugi Otok, water in three shades of turquoise, pines down to the shore. The photos are accurate. The crescent is around 800 metres long, the shallows run out for a good 350 metres over pale sand, and the pinewood behind the beach is old Aleppo pine that throws real shade at midday. It is the closest thing the Adriatic has to a Caribbean lagoon, and small children can wade further out than their parents can throw a ball.

Why the boat wins

What the photos leave out is the journey. By land, Sakarun means a ferry to Brbinj, a drive across the island and a walk down from the car park, in company with several hundred people making the same trip. By boat from Zadar it is a straight run across the channel: you anchor in the bay itself, swim to the sand from the stern, and when the midday crowd builds you simply move along to the next cove. No car can follow you there.

The colour, by the way, has a simple explanation: the seabed is fine white sand for hundreds of metres, and it bounces light back through the shallow water. No filters involved.

The lighthouse and the hundred thousand yolks

Round the corner from Sakarun, at the island's northwestern tip, stands the Veli Rat lighthouse. Built in 1849 under Austro-Hungarian rule, it is, at 42 metres, the tallest lighthouse on the Adriatic. Its facade is a warm ochre yellow, and here the story gets good: according to local tradition, the builders mixed around one hundred thousand egg yolks into the plaster to make it withstand the salt wind. Whether the recipe is fact or legend, the paint has outlasted empires, and the lighthouse still works. On a speedboat trip it makes the perfect photo stop after the beach.

When to go

June and September give you the bay at its best: warm sea, empty sand, low light. In July and August, arrive before eleven or stay after four; the middle of the day belongs to the excursion boats, and although the bay is big enough to share, the early morning there is a different experience altogether, and you will have earned it by sleeping aboard.

How to fit Sakarun into your trip

Honesty first: on a one-day sailing trip from Zadar, a sailboat does not reach Sakarun; the distance needs more time than an eight-hour day allows. There are two good ways in. Fast: our speedboat trips do Sakarun, the Veli Rat lighthouse and a lunch stop in one day, and you are back in Zadar for dinner. Slow: on the three-day sailing route, Sakarun is the closing chapter, the morning of day three, after the Kornati and a night at anchor in Telašćica. Arriving at that beach with the day's first light, before a single excursion boat has crossed the channel, is the kind of moment people book entire holidays around.
