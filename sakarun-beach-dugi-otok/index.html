Sakarun Beach: Getting to Croatia’s White-Sand Bay by Boat

Every summer, photos of Sakarun beach do the rounds: a horseshoe of white sand on Dugi Otok, water in three shades of turquoise, pine trees down to the shore. The photos are accurate. What they leave out is the journey.

Why you should arrive by boat

By road, Sakarun means a ferry to Brbinj, a drive across the island and a walk down from the parking lot, with several hundred other visitors making the same trip. By boat from Zadar, it is a straight run across the channel. You anchor in the bay itself, swim to the sand from the transom, and when the afternoon crowd builds, you simply move to the next cove. Nobody with a car can follow you there.

When to go

June and September give you the bay at its best: warm sea, empty sand, and the low light that makes the shallows glow. In July and August, arrive before 11:00 or after 16:00. The middle of the day belongs to the excursion boats, and the bay is big enough to share, but early mornings there are something else entirely.

What the water is actually like

The bay is shallow for a long way out, over pale sand, which is what produces that Caribbean colour. Children can wade for fifty metres. The seabed is kind to anchors and kinder to snorkelers, and the pines behind the beach hold a bar or two in season.

Combining Sakarun with the rest of Dugi Otok

Sakarun sits at the northwest tip of Dugi Otok. On our three-day sailing route it is the closing chapter: Kornati first, a night in Telašćica, then Sakarun on the morning of day three. On a speedboat, you can do Sakarun, the Veli Rat lighthouse and a lunch stop in one day and still be back in Zadar for dinner.
