Zadar Old Town: Two Thousand Years on a Peninsula Half a Kilometre Wide

Every trip we run starts and ends within sight of the same small peninsula, half a kilometre wide, packed with the leftovers of everyone who ever wanted it. Before you get on a boat, it is worth understanding what you are sailing away from.

The forum, and the column of shame

Zadar's Roman forum was laid out between the 1st century BC and the 3rd century AD: temples, colonnades, a capitolium for Jupiter, Juno and Minerva. Most of it is now a wide field of carved stone you can walk across for free, which is unusual enough on its own. One full column still stands, and its later career is better than its first: in the Middle Ages it served as a pillar of shame, where offenders were chained up for the town to look at.

A church made of a forum

In the 9th century someone built a church directly on the forum, using its stones. St Donatus is round, tall, austere and completely unlike anything else on this coast; look at the base and you can see Roman blocks and inscriptions built straight into the walls. It has not held regular services for a long time. Instead its acoustics made it a concert hall, which is a fair second act for a building that started as recycled Roman masonry.

Walls that made the UNESCO list

Venice fortified Zadar heavily against the Ottomans, and the result is now part of a UNESCO World Heritage listing covering Venetian defensive works. The Land Gate of 1543 is the showpiece: the grandest of the surviving gates, and still the main way into the old town on foot. At the peninsula's southern end, Five Wells Square sits on top of the cistern that let the city survive being besieged.

And then the sea

Walk the length of the peninsula and it ends at the waterfront, where two modern installations do the same job the forum once did: bring everyone to the same place at the same hour. The Sea Organ and the sunset are the reason the promenade fills up every evening.

The best view of all of it, though, is from thirty metres offshore: the walls, the campaniles, the promenade and the sun going down behind Ugljan, in one frame. That is what our evening sail is for, and why it is the trip most guests book last and remember first.
